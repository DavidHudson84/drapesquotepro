Where the money goes

The cleaning is the easy bit.

A curtain job leaks margin everywhere except the wash. The quote that never got chased, the takedown booked across town from where the van already was, the panel that came back from the plant with nobody sure which window it belonged to.

01

Quotes that go quiet

Most quotes aren't lost on price. They're lost because nobody followed them up, and by the time someone does, the customer has booked whoever rang back first.

02

Items that go missing

Twelve panels come down off four windows, go into bags, through two processes and onto a rack. Without a record against every item, the rehang is guesswork and the one missing hook becomes a phone call.

03

Claims you can't defend

Faded linings, sun rot and old water marks were there before you touched them. If nobody photographed them before takedown, it's your word against the customer's, and the insurer goes with theirs.

How it works

One job, one record, start to finish.

Every stage reads and writes the same job, so what the office quoted is what the van takes down, what the plant cleans and what goes back up.

  1. The enquiry comes in

    Your website quote form lands in Drapr with the customer's measurements and photos, and the Enquiries tab shows how many are still untouched.

  2. Quote it in a couple of minutes

    Room by room, width and drop, on a phone or at the desk. Drapr picks the right price tier off your own list, adds mould treatment and repairs, and shows ex and inc GST as you type.

  3. Send it, and let them accept online

    A branded quote PDF goes out by email with an Accept online button. When the customer accepts, the job confirms itself and the office is told.

  4. Chase the ones that don't

    A three-step follow-up ladder (nudge, check-in, last chance) tells you who's due today, and a chase list lands in your inbox three mornings a week.

  5. Book the takedown where the van already is

    Drapr reads the next fortnight of the calendar and suggests three windows worth offering, near jobs you've already got booked. It suggests; the customer still picks.

  6. Inspect before anything comes down

    A pre-inspection checklist, a 1–10 condition score and up to eight photos, turned into a branded report the customer gets a copy of.

  7. Track every item through the plant

    The quote becomes a list of items. Takedown ticks them off, production records method, bag number and storage location, and anything found on site gets added and priced.

  8. Rehang from a sheet that knows where it all is

    The printed rehang sheet carries the bag and rack location the plant entered, with no prices on it, so it can go on site and into the plant.

  9. Invoice through your job system

    Accepted jobs are raised in Fergus automatically, matched to the right customer and site, and flow through to Xero from there. Nothing is typed twice.

  10. Ask for the review

    When the job is marked rehung, the customer gets a thank-you email with a Google review button. The happy customers are the ones who never think to leave one.

What it does

Built around how curtain work actually runs.

Not a generic field-service app with a curtain template on it. Drapr knows the difference between a sheer and a velvet, prices a pelmet by width and a blind by the square metre, and expects the job to be done from a phone in a driveway.

01 — Quoting

Your price list, applied the same way every time.

Rates live in one place and every new quote uses them. Enter width and drop in metres or millimetres and Drapr selects the tier, so two staff measuring the same window quote the same price. Change a rate and it's logged in the price change history.

  • Curtains by track width, tiered by drop; sheers and velvets on their own tiers
  • Blinds by the square metre; pelmets, swags and valances by width
  • Mould treatment, repairs and hardware as add-ons with quantities
  • Insurer and claim number on the quote for insurance work
  • Your terms of trade printed on every quote

02 — Winning the work

The follow-up nobody gets around to, done.

Every open quote sits on a ladder. At ten days it's a nudge, at three weeks a check-in offering to stage the job, at five weeks a last chance. Drapr drafts each one in your wording, you press send, and a week after the last one it offers the quote up as lost so your pipeline stays honest.

Three mornings a week a chase list arrives by email with the quotes due and a call list for the ones worth a phone call instead.

03 — Booking and the van

Where's the van this fortnight?

Nobody can hold two weeks of a calendar in their head. When a quote is accepted, Drapr reads the bookings already in Fergus, ranks the days by where the van is already going, and says it in plain words: three windows worth offering the customer on the phone.

On the day, the driver gets a run sheet built for one hand at a front door. Tap the address to navigate, tap the number to call, and the booking notes (gate codes, "call when five minutes away") are shown word for word.

04 — Items and production

Every panel accounted for.

Once accepted, the quote becomes a list of items, and every stage after that works off the same list. Takedown records what came down and its condition. Production records the cleaning method, the bag number and the storage location, and flags any issue. Rehang ticks it back up.

  • Takedown and rehang sheets printed with no prices on them
  • Item found on site? Add it there and then, priced off the list
  • Bag numbering, storage locations and item tags set up to suit your plant
  • Activity log of who did what, and when

05 — Inspections and reports

Proof of condition, before and after.

The pre-inspection records mould, fading, fraying, water marks and track damage with notes, a condition score and photos, before anything is touched. The post-inspection does the same on the way back. Each becomes a branded PDF report, or one combined report, emailed straight to the customer or the insurer.

It's the document that settles the "it wasn't like that before" conversation, and on insurance work it's what the assessor wants to see.

Use cases

The jobs a curtain cleaner actually gets.

Retail is the easy one. Drapr was built for the work that comes through someone else: an insurer, a dry cleaner's counter, a property manager or a blind shop.

Retail

The lounge-room job

A homeowner fills in the form on your website with rough measurements. The office quotes it that afternoon, the customer accepts online that night, and the takedown is offered on a day the van is already in their suburb. After the rehang they get a thank-you and a Google review link.

Insurance

The smoke or flood claim

The quote carries the insurer and claim number. The pre-inspection photographs every panel before it comes down, the post-inspection shows the result, and the combined report goes to the builder or assessor as one PDF. Mould treatment and non-restorable items are itemised, not argued about later.

Trade accounts

Work through someone else's counter

Dry cleaners take curtains over the counter, restoration companies send packouts, fit-out firms send retail stores. Each is an account with its own reference, whether that's a docket number, a job number or a WR number. It goes on the quote and the invoice, so their accounts team can match it without ringing you.

Commercial

One customer, many sites

Aged care, hotels, real estate offices and restaurants bill as one account while the site changes per job. An aged care facility can be quoted room by room and staged over several visits without losing track of which wing is done.

Partners

Selling cleaning through a blind shop

A window furnishing retailer quotes your cleaning alongside their new blinds, under their own logo, on your prices. They choose whether you or they do the takedown and who bills the customer, and their commission is worked out for them and statemented on the first of each month.

In the plant

Forty panels, one hotel floor

Big jobs are where items go missing. Every panel is an item with a bag number and a rack location, and the rehang sheet tells the installer exactly which window each one goes back on.

What Drapr isn't.

It isn't your accounting system. Invoicing goes through Fergus and into Xero. Drapr raises the job and hands it over; it doesn't replace either.

It's built around Fergus today. The calendar, job push and invoicing assume Fergus. If you run something else, talk to us. Quoting, items, inspections and follow-ups work without it, but the joined-up part is where most of the value is.

It's early outside our own business. Drapr has run Dr Drapes every day for months, and trade partners are using the partner portal now. Setting it up for another curtain cleaner is something we'll do with a small number of operators first, carefully, and those operators get a real say in what comes next.

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

Can I use my own prices?

Yes. The price list is yours, set up by category: curtains, sheers and velvets by width with tiers by drop, blinds by the square metre, pelmets by width, and repairs by quantity. Change a rate and new quotes use it straight away; quotes already sent keep the price they went out at.

Do I have to use Fergus?

Today, the booking suggestions, run sheet and automatic job creation read and write Fergus. If you use a different job system, get in touch and tell us which one. That's exactly the conversation we want to have with early operators.

Will my customers see Drapr, or my business?

Your business. Quotes, reports, emails and the accept page carry your logo and details. The Drapr name stays inside the app.

How does online acceptance work?

The quote email has an Accept online button that opens a page showing the quote and your terms. The customer accepts there, with no login needed. The job moves to confirmed, the office is notified, the customer gets a confirmation, and the job is raised in Fergus.

Can trade partners quote my work?

Yes. Partners get their own login to a partner portal. They quote on your price list (they can't change it), do the pre-inspection, send the quote to their customer with their logo beside yours, and follow the job through. You set each partner's commission, and statements go out on the first of each month.

Does it work for insurance work?

It was built alongside a restoration business, so yes. Insurer and claim number go on the quote, pre- and post-inspection reports with photos go to the builder or assessor, and restoration companies can be set up as trade accounts with their own job number on every quote and invoice.

What does it cost?

That gets settled with the first operators who use it rather than decided at them. Get in touch and we'll talk it through properly.
